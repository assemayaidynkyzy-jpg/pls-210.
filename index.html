Research question

Since Kazakhstan's Supreme Court ruling in September 2024 that barred the exclusion of individual hijab-wearing students, how has enforcement of the hijab restriction in Kazakhstan's schools changed through 2026?

Trigger

The ruling that removed one enforcement tool

Question type

An open "how", not a yes/no bet

Where

All schools, public and private

Until

Now, the edge of what is documentable

Empirical, not normative: it asks how enforcement changed, not whether it should have.

Epistemological positioning

Which kind of study is this?

Positivist

Tests a general cause-and-effect law across many cases.

Not this: I claim no universal law

Interpretivist

Reads what something means to the people living it.

Not this: I don't interpret meanings or motives

Descriptive

Documents what actually happened, with verifiable records.

This: court rulings, ministry statements, licensing records

Answerable either way: a documented change and a finding that nothing moved are both complete results.

Contribution

What this study adds

01

How states adapt when a court removes a tool

A pattern documented in other legal systems, not yet traced through this case.

02

How far words run ahead of action

An official naming a security agency or threatening licenses is not the same as a confirmed inspection or order.

03

A case the literature hasn't reached

No scholarship I found covers enforcement in Kazakhstan after the 2024 ruling.

Literature review · mechanism

How states route around a ruling

Limit: Schotel and Varol draw on European and American cases, so I use them here as mechanisms to test, not as proof they apply to Kazakhstan.

Literature review · context

Why "secularism" is the automatic justification

Mechanism explains how the toolbox changes. Context explains why it is always justified the same way. Neither proves what happened: only the evidence can.

Categories and typology

Enforcement is a toolbox, not a relay race

"The shift" is a change in which tools are in the box, not one tool handed to the next.
